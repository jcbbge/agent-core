## F1 — Candidate NEW item: movement as a first-class object (time axis)

**Status:** not in STAGING. Candidate for a new STG.

STG-674 and STG-675 are both **space** axes: whose it is, where it is — both
answering "right now." The operator's session framing (2026-08-21) described a
capability neither can serve and neither should try to:

> "she needs that ability to manage individual catalog items, like chess pieces,
> where she can move things around… If she has 100 tables at one event for one
> day, and then there's an event tomorrow that uses 10 tables, she doesn't have
> to move the tables after the end of the first event back to the main warehouse
> only to send the tables back to the next day's event."

And on zoom level:

> "she needs to zoom out and see not just a single event, but arranged event,
> whether it's one day, three days, or the full year."

**The distinction: placement is a state; movement is an event.** Today
`catalog_item_allocations.quantity` is overwritten in place (verified in
`packages/db/src/schema/stock-locations.ts`), so a move leaves no trace and no
future-dated question can be asked.

**Critical refinement from the operator:** the leave-behind is **time-boxed** —
*"it can only be for that specific day, or perhaps even an event that's a few
days out."* It needs placement **with an expiry**, not merely placement at a
date. Without the expiry, stock left behind silently becomes that venue's par
and the ledger rots.

**Why this belongs to the index, not U2:** it is very likely where the lookahead
heat map (STG-673, U1) and the in-a-night flip sheet (STG-676) actually meet —
both are reading one future-placement timeline at different zoom levels. If each
unit builds its own, Arc gets two.

**Dependency direction:** this item depends on STG-674 and STG-675 (you cannot
schedule a move until you can say which pool moved and which physical location
it left). They do **not** depend on it. Both are safely promotable first.

## F2 — Program-level principle: the movement cost asymmetry

**Status:** belongs to the principles thread (STG-677 / STG-678), not to U2.

The operator's metaphor, 2026-08-21:

> "preparing a ship to sail across the Atlantic. You can't send it off and be
> like, oh yeah, I forgot something, and then turn around… once that maiden
> voyage is set on, there's no turning back."

**The asymmetry is real but NOT free** (operator clarification, same session —
this corrects an earlier over-strong reading of the metaphor):

> "there is a taxable cost for over-packing. extra weight. extra expense of
> time. extra effort… its allowed and okay, but i dont want to create the
> affordances to create shortcuts. i would rather pave the optimal paths so its
> never a concern."

**The principle, stated correctly:**

1. A forgotten item costs a return voyage — crew, fuel, hours, and Maggie's
   standing. That remains the worse failure.
2. Over-packing is **cheaper, not free**: weight, time, labour, and space at the
   far end.
3. Therefore over-packing is a **permitted fallback, never a paved path.** Do
   not build affordances that make "add extra just in case" the easy button. An
   easy button there teaches the operator to stop planning — which manufactures
   the very dependency STG-677/678 warn against, and degrades the awareness pass
   that is the actual skill.
4. **The design target is precision, and precision is bought with confidence.**
   People pad because they do not trust the number. Make the needed quantity
   knowable — accurate, current, and visibly dated — and padding stops being
   reached for. Confidence is the product; padding is the symptom of its
   absence.

**Cross-reference:** this is why STG-675 requires staleness surfaced on every
physical quantity. A trusted, dated number is what makes precision cheaper than
insurance. F2 and that done-when are one argument.

This constrains multiple threads and should be recorded once, centrally.
